# Performance Tracker — Private Health Intelligence Alpha

## Purpose

Build a private, single-user health-data intelligence app for Daniel. It connects existing wearable and workout data, preserves the source data, and gives an AI coach enough context to recommend practical actions toward the user's goals.

This is **not** another manual logging app. Wearables and workout platforms remain the primary source of measurement. Optional user input is short, structured, and only used when the data alone cannot explain a meaningful event or trend.

The private alpha may later be shared with close friends and family, but it is not designed as a public or multi-tenant product yet.

## Product principles

- **Decision support over dashboards.** The primary output is a clear recommendation, its evidence, confidence, caveats, and reassessment date.
- **Source-aware data.** Preserve provider, original identifier, timestamps, time zone, sync time, and raw payload reference for every imported record.
- **No double counting.** A workout or metric mirrored across providers must be linked or deduplicated without deleting provenance.
- **Minimal input.** Ask only for structured context that cannot be inferred from connected data, such as illness, injury/pain, alcohol, travel, exceptional stress, or a medication/supplement change.
- **Wellness boundary.** The coach may support wellbeing and performance decisions; it must not diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment, or advise medication changes.
- **Private by default.** Store secrets and health data securely; provide explicit connection, disconnect, export, and deletion controls before inviting other users.

## Alpha scope

### In scope

1. Connect Huawei Health and Hevy.
2. Import historical data and keep it current incrementally.
3. Normalize health measurements and detailed strength workouts into a source-aware timeline.
4. Capture optional structured context through a small form.
5. Provide an AI coach that can explain a recommendation using the user's own data.
6. Establish data quality, sync-status, and consent controls needed before a small private beta.

### Out of scope for this alpha

- Public signup, billing, teams, social features, or coach marketplaces.
- Clinical diagnosis, emergency triage, treatment plans, or medical-record integrations.
- A general-purpose food/calorie diary.
- Automatic ingestion from every wearable platform.
- Unsupervised changes to a user's workout programme.

## Alpha connectors

### 1. Huawei Health — primary wearable connection

**Target device:** `huawei gt6 pro`

**Objective:** retrieve the health and activity data exposed by Huawei Health under Daniel's Huawei ID and user-approved scopes.

**Integration approach**

- Register a Huawei developer application and enable Health Kit.
- Use Huawei ID authentication and Health Kit authorization.
- Request only the scopes justified by the alpha: activity/steps, workouts where exposed, sleep, heart rate/resting heart rate, SpO₂, stress, and body temperature where available.
- Treat device- and scope-specific availability as variable. The `huawei gt6 pro` must be validated against Daniel's real account; do not assume every Health Kit data type is available or historical.
- Keep a per-metric coverage record: requested scope, granted scope, earliest available timestamp, latest imported timestamp, source device, and sync outcome.

**Proof-of-concept acceptance criteria**

- Daniel can authenticate with Huawei ID and explicitly authorize the app.
- The connector imports at least 30 days of available data without exposing credentials in logs.
- The alpha displays exactly which requested metrics and historical ranges were actually received.
- The sync process preserves source timestamps and prevents duplicate imports on rerun.
- Missing or revoked permission appears as a clear connection-state error, not as a false zero value.

**Known validation risks**

- Huawei documentation describes different availability by watch model, operating system, Health Kit scope, and user monitoring settings.
- Certain metrics may only be available as current-day summaries or may have restricted historical data.
- Restricted health scopes may require Huawei review before use.

**References**

- https://developer.huawei.com/consumer/en/hms/huaweihealth/
- https://developer.huawei.com/consumer/en/doc/hmscore-guides/obtain-smart-watch-data-0000001365189921

### 2. Hevy — detailed strength-training connection

**Objective:** ingest workout, exercise, set, routine, and body-measurement data to give the AI coach the training-load and progressive-overload context not available in most wearable summaries.

**Integration approach**

- Use a Hevy Pro API key generated by Daniel in Hevy developer settings.
- Encrypt the key at rest; never write it to Git, application logs, analytics, prompts, or error reports.
- Backfill workout history once, then consume the workout-events endpoint incrementally for changes and deletions.
- Import workouts with exercises, sets, load, repetitions, duration, notes, and workout timestamps when exposed by the API.
- Prefer Hevy as the canonical detailed source for strength sessions. If the same session appears in Huawei Health, link the records rather than summing volume or duration twice.

**Proof-of-concept acceptance criteria**

- A valid Hevy Pro key verifies the connected account without printing the key.
- The connector imports a historical sample of completed workouts and their sets.
- A new or edited Hevy workout is reflected through incremental sync.
- The app can calculate at least: sessions per week, exercise volume, load/reps trends, and days since each muscle group was trained.
- An invalid or revoked key results in a reconnect-required state without data loss.

**Known validation risks**

- Hevy's public API is restricted to Hevy Pro and is explicitly early-stage; data models or endpoints can change.
- Hevy uses an API key rather than consumer OAuth, so secret storage and rotation are an explicit alpha requirement.

**Reference**

- https://api.hevyapp.com/docs

## Canonical data model for the alpha

The implementation must keep raw provider payloads separately from normalized entities.

| Entity | Minimum fields | Initial source |
|---|---|---|
| `connection` | provider, connection status, granted scopes, last sync, error state | Huawei Health, Hevy |
| `measurement` | metric, value, unit, observed-at, source, source ID, device, imported-at | Huawei Health |
| `daily_summary` | date, steps, distance, active energy, source, coverage | Huawei Health |
| `sleep_session` | start/end, duration, available stages, source, coverage | Huawei Health |
| `workout` | start/end, type, duration, source, source ID, deduplication link | Huawei Health, Hevy |
| `strength_set` | workout ID, exercise, set number, reps, load, unit, RPE if present | Hevy |
| `body_measurement` | date, metric, value, unit, source, source ID | Hevy; later providers |
| `context_event` | type, occurred-at, structured value, optional note, user confirmation | App form |
| `coach_recommendation` | goal, recommendation, evidence IDs, confidence, caveats, reassess-at, outcome | App |

## AI coach requirements

The coach is a decision-support layer over the normalized timeline. Every recommendation must include:

1. **Action:** a concrete next step or an explicit recommendation to hold steady.
2. **Evidence:** linked measurements, workouts, and/or context events with dates.
3. **Reasoning:** why those data support the action.
4. **Confidence:** high, medium, or low, based on data coverage and consistency.
5. **Caveats:** missing data, conflicting signals, or non-medical safety boundary.
6. **Reassessment:** the date, condition, or next data event that will evaluate the recommendation.

The coach must distinguish factual readings from inference. It may propose small experiments, but it must not claim causality until there is enough evidence.

## Later connectors — requirements, not alpha work

### Apple Health

- Requires a native iOS component using HealthKit; it is not a server-to-server Apple Health API.
- Must request fine-grained user consent for each required data type and handle limited historical access.
- The iOS client must upload user-authorized records securely and incrementally to the backend.
- Initial desired data types: workouts, sleep, heart-rate signals, steps, active energy, and body measurements.
- Must retain HealthKit source metadata to avoid duplicate imports from Oura, Hevy, or Huawei.

Reference: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data

### Oura

- Register an Oura API application and implement OAuth 2.0; personal access tokens are not a supported production approach.
- On connection, backfill an agreed historical window, then use Oura webhooks for updates.
- Initial desired data: sleep, readiness, resilience, activity, workouts, sessions, heart rate, stress, SpO₂, and VO₂ max where consented and available.
- Plan for Oura's app-approval threshold before expanding beyond a small private beta.

Reference: https://cloud.ouraring.com/v2/docs

### ABC Trainerize

- Verify that the controlling business account is on a Studio or Enterprise plan; direct API and webhook access are unavailable on lower plans.
- Confirm the product can receive data under the correct client/business relationship and consent model.
- Initial desired data: completed workouts, programmes, goals, habits, body stats, and appointments.
- Prefer webhooks for workout completion and goal/habit changes, with reconciliation/backfill for missed events.
- Do not make Trainerize an alpha dependency until exact account entitlement and API documentation access are confirmed.

Reference: https://help.trainerize.com/hc/en-us/articles/37082084919060-Using-API-and-Webhooks-With-ABC-Trainerize

## Definition of success for the private alpha

- Huawei Health and Hevy have reliable, observable connection states.
- The app presents a unified timeline without silently double-counting data.
- The AI coach produces one useful, evidence-backed, non-medical recommendation from real connected data.
- Daniel can understand data provenance, disconnect a provider, and delete imported data.
